## 2. What this means for the open question ("how does the backend handle an uncertain `stop`")

- **It fails closed and changes nothing.** A stop that times out at 15 s, three times in a row, leaves the session in the terminal state it already committed, with `teardownStatus PENDING` and the request kept. The backend never marks the teardown done on a timeout, and it never releases a slot (the slot is the runner's; the backend holds none). [V] steps 5-6.
- **It is self-healing while the window lasts.** Every 10 s tick re-queues any unconfirmed terminal session whose `expires_at` is within 15 minutes, with the same idempotency key, so it joins a close the runner is already running. [V] step 8. Existing tests cover the re-send, the join key and the CONFIRMED CAS: the stop-stub cases in `GuidedServiceTransportFaultIT` (39 cases, green in `obs009-b-04`). [T]
- **Nothing in this path is unsafe in the sense of D5 e** ("if the behaviour is unsafe it goes to @architect"). No state, ledger, action or evidence write depends on the stop's outcome. So I raise nothing as unsafe.

## 3. Observations for @architect (availability, not safety; no action taken)

1. **The teardown executor is small and sheds silently.** Two daemon threads, a queue of 64, `DiscardPolicy` (`session/GuidedSessionService.java:2794-2807`). One wedged session occupies a thread for up to about 45 s (3 sends x 15 s). With three or more wedged sessions at once, the others wait behind them, and past 64 queued attempts new ones are dropped without a log line. The durable request survives and the next 10 s tick re-queues it, so no stop is lost inside the window, but the delay is not bounded by the 15 s ceiling alone. [V code; the combined timing is [I], not run.]
2. **After 15 minutes the backend stops trying.** A session whose runner never confirms stays `PENDING` forever, visible only as a count in one log line every 15 minutes. This is the accepted H8 shape (`aria-warn-ruling01`), restated here because D5-1/D5-2 (bounded close, reply after settle) make a confirmation inside the window the normal case, and a session still `PENDING` after the window now points at a runner that did not answer at all. [I]
3. **The stop reason the runner receives depends on the terminal state.** With LR a (this lane), a context lifecycle `EXPIRED` now ends the session `EXPIRED` and the durable stop request carries `reason EXPIRED` (previously `FAILED` and `UNKNOWN_OUTCOME`). That matches what `assignmentLifecycle` always sent. Pinned in `GuidedLifecycleReasonIT` (`teardownReason`). [V]
